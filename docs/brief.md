## 5. Content priorities

1. **First screen (desktop and phone):** name, student status, direction, a one-line availability note, and visible proof (the result itself, not a button to see it).
2. **Three or four strong examples** with the headline result always visible and the "how" in optional detail: Caterpillar cost analysis, supplier digitization pilots, CoffeeChat AI, and mentoring/people.
3. **People and growth:** mentoring and a short personal story, with detail in expanders.
4. **Compact background:** one line per role. The résumé holds the detail.
5. **Availability and contact:** clear, factual and dated, never "career on pause."

**Cut:** slogans, repeated summaries, long per-role descriptions, the skills grid, "Learning now" lists.

## 6. Design principles

- Results are visible without hover or a click. Expandable detail uses native `<details>` so it works on touch and with screen readers.
- A few strong examples rather than a long timeline or a grid of similar cards.
- Warm and personal, but credible: portrait, first-person voice, specific moments.
- Diagrams over charts for employer work. Every diagram gets Toby's confidentiality review before going public.
- Evergreen copy: absolute dates instead of "currently", "now" or "this semester". The site must stay accurate for two years without edits.
- Stay lightweight: plain HTML, CSS and JS, no build step, light and dark themes, works at 375px width.

## 7. Success criteria

- A recruiter can answer "who is this, what has he done, when is he available, how do I reach him" in **under 10 seconds** on a phone.
- Main page text is **300–350 words visible by default** (baseline: ~1,230), with detail available on demand.
- Every claim on the launched site appears in §3a with matching wording, and matches the résumé PDF.
- The page still reads accurately in December 2028 with no edits.
- At least two concrete stories show listening, working with people and learning from feedback, not just analysis.
- Passes a basic accessibility check: headings, contrast, keyboard use, alt text, no hover-only content.

## 9. Visual identity (Prototype B design pass)

- **Palette (revised after review):** main background `#faf9f6`, soft ivory `#f1ede5` only for section contrast (at-a-glance panel, cost illustration, mentoring band). Earlier: warm paper `#f4efe6` and teal-ink `#13262a` (continuity with the current site), graphite `#4b5758` for secondary text, warm hairlines `#cdc3b1`, and **one accent: seal vermilion `#b23b25`**. It refers to a Chinese name seal (a personal signature) and to red-pen markup on drawings and cost sheets. The generic SaaS blue is retired. All text pairs pass WCAG AA in light and dark modes.
- **Type:** Newsreader (serif, name and headings: a warm, human voice), IBM Plex Sans (body; engineered, industrial heritage), IBM Plex Mono (labels and dates in a spec-sheet / drawing title-block style). Self-hosted, SIL OFL; licenses in `prototypes/fonts/`.
- **Language (revised after review):** sentence-case labels in the sans; mono only for dates; fewer rules. Originally: hairline rules and spec-sheet tables instead of rounded cards and pills; a red seal mark; one illustration per piece of work; numbered section labels.
- **Composition:** the Caterpillar feature carries the most weight (narrative + should-cost illustration + pilot tools); CoffeeChat flips the layout; mentoring is a tinted typographic band; "My path" is a route line whose dashed red section marks the future.
- **Interaction:** native `<details>` with a short height transition where supported; essentials are visible without interaction; 44px minimum touch targets; visible focus; reduced motion respected.
- **Review layer:** `prototypes/b/review.html` (generated) holds the highlights, confirmation list and private claim boundaries. The visitor version contains none of these.
